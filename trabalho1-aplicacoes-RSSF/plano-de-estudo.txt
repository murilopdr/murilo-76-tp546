WSN FOR INDUSTRIAL CONDITION MONITORING - STUDY PLAN
======================================================
Target length: ~5 pages (excluding references)

1. INTRODUCTION (~0.5 p)
------------------------
- Define WSN briefly.
- State the chosen application: WSN for industrial condition
  monitoring / predictive maintenance.
- One paragraph outlining the rest of the paper.
References: [1] Akyildiz et al. (2002) - definitional citation.
            [12] Kaur et al. (2025) - to show the field is still
            active/growing (pair with [1] so the paper doesn't read
            as dated).

2. APPLICATION AND MOTIVATION (~1.25 p)
----------------------------------------
2.1 Scenario
    - Rotating machinery (motors, pumps, bearings, compressors)
      monitored continuously to detect degradation before failure.
    References: [7] Zonta et al. (2020).

2.2 Why a Wireless Sensor Network
    - Cabling cost and installation time in a plant.
    - Machinery that rotates or moves.
    - Retrofitting existing equipment.
    - Ability to instrument non-critical assets that would not
      justify wired sensors.
    References: [1] Akyildiz et al. (2002) - general WSN advantages.
                [12] Kaur et al. (2025) - energy/real-time framing.

2.3 Requirements
    - Reliability under harsh electromagnetic conditions.
    - Long node lifetime.
    - Bounded latency.
    - Tolerance to metal-rich multipath.
    References: [8] RF-Powered Low-Energy Sensor Nodes (2021) -
                electromagnetically harsh industrial environments.
                [9] Heidari et al. (2024) - reliability/availability
                under permanent faults.

3. NETWORK IMPLEMENTATION (~1.75 p)
-------------------------------------
3.1 Architecture
    - Sensor nodes -> routers/mesh -> gateway -> plant server /
      edge processing.
    References: [3] WirelessHART survey (2021).

3.2 Node Hardware
    - MCU, transceiver, antenna, power supply, sensing front-end.
    - Local pre-processing (e.g., on-node FFT to avoid streaming
      raw vibration data).
    References: [8] RF-Powered Low-Energy Sensor Nodes (2021).

3.3 Communication Standards
    - IEEE 802.15.4-based radios in the 2.4 GHz ISM band.
    - WirelessHART (IEC 62591) and ISA100.11a (ANSI/ISA100.11a-2011).
    - Also mention ZigBee and WIA-PA.
    References: [2] IEEE 802.15.4-2020.
                [3] WirelessHART survey (2021).
                [4] IIoT WirelessHART/ISA100.11a comparison
                (verify venue/year before citing).
                [5] IEC 62591.
                [6] ANSI/ISA-100.11a-2011.

3.4 Energy Management
    - Duty cycling.
    - On-node feature extraction.
    - The battery-replacement problem.
    - Energy harvesting as a mitigation.
    References: [8] RF-Powered Low-Energy Sensor Nodes (2021) -
                eliminating periodic battery replacement.
                [7] Zonta et al. (2020).

3.5 Interference and Coexistence
    - Shared 2.4 GHz band.
    - Clear channel assessment and channel blacklisting as
      standardized mitigations.
    References: [3] WirelessHART survey (2021) - stochastic delays,
                packet drop, external noise.

4. SENSORS (~1 p)
------------------
- Accelerometer/vibration (primary modality: bearing wear,
  misalignment, imbalance).
- Temperature.
- Current/power (motor current).
- Acoustic emission.
- Pressure, humidity.
- Note sampling-rate implications: vibration needs kHz-range
  sampling, driving the energy and bandwidth budget, unlike
  temperature (Hz range).
- Optional: summary table (modality | measured quantity |
  typical sampling rate).
References: [7] Zonta et al. (2020) - condition monitoring context.
            [9] Heidari et al. (2024).

5. PROPOSED ADVANCEMENTS: NOISE MODULATION (~0.5 p)
------------------------------------------------------
- Frame as emerging, not deployed.
- Energy harvesting and battery-free nodes as the direction of
  travel.
- Noise modulation (NoiseMod) as a candidate physical-layer
  technique.
- State its limits explicitly:
    - Short-range only (energy crossover distance depends on
      carrier frequency).
    - Fading error floor without diversity.
    - Low data rate -- suited to status/temperature reporting,
      not raw vibration streaming.
References: [10] Basar (2023) - NoiseMod origin.
            [11] de Figueiredo, Pereira, Vilas Boas, Garcia,
            Zayyani, de Souza (2026) - crossover-distance/energy
            trade-off analysis (essential counterweight).
            [13] Survey on Noise-Based Communication (2025).

6. CONCLUSION (~0.25 p)
-------------------------
- Summarize application, implementation, and sensing discussion.
- Close with the outlook given by noise modulation.
References: none needed (synthesis of prior sections).
